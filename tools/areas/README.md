# areas

Scripts for a game's areas: the area tables, the scripts that run in them, their maps, and the exits and fast-travel routes between them.

| file | purpose |
|---|---|
| `areatable.py` | Builds a title's area table out of its own `ECL` scripts -- one row per script with the disk side that carries it, the `GEO`s it loads and the square it puts an arriving party on -- which is what produced `goldbox.areas.AREAS_SILVER_BLADES` for `#20 (Build an area table for Silver Blades)` and `goldbox.areas.AREAS_CURSE` for `#192 (Convert a Curse of the Azure Bonds DOS save into a C64 one, which the importer refuses today)`. Nothing is carried across from Pool of Radiance: the opcode tables come out of that title's own `DUNGEON` through `newecl.py`'s self-modifying dispatch, and the script load address is derived from the scripts themselves, since the five `GOTO`s at the head of every one name addresses inside it and exactly one page boundary puts all of them inside every script -- `$9900` in Pool of Radiance, `$8000` in Silver Blades. The disk and the arrival square are a **forward propagation over each script's own control-flow graph** rather than a sweep in address order, so a `NEWECL` reports only what is true on every path that reaches it; sweeping instead both invents a square (Silver Blades' `ECL30` hands its entry-4 square to a `NEWECL` no path reaches from there) and loses one (Pool of Radiance's `ECL1B` writes two, because the Kobold Caves have two entrances). The same propagation carries the indoors flag to each `LOADFILES`, because the first operand names a `SQRDATA` and not a `GEO` when that flag is zero -- 5 of 5 such loads in the three titles are Pool of Radiance's three wilderness windows, and reading them as maps had `ECL19` claiming `GEO04`, a castle floor on a side it never asks for. `--check` re-derives a title's whole table and diffs it against what `goldbox/areas.py` ships, field by field: on 2026-09-08 Curse agreed 25 of 25 and Silver Blades 22 of 22 on id, side and maps, and Pool of Radiance -- the control, whose squares were recorded from driven arrivals -- agreed on 30 of 30 sides, 27 of 30 map columns and 10 of the 11 arrival squares the walk will name at all. Prints a string operand as its length and never as text. |
| `ecl6derefs.py` | For #445 (The game's third fight outcome, THE PARTY RUNS AWAY, has never been seen on a screen): every reference to $6DE0-$6DEF in Pool of Radiance's thirty area scripts, by the control-flow walk and by a raw byte scan that marks anything NOT WALKED, plus every SAVETABLE base and index that could reach $6DE6. Reads the player's C64 disks through `eclcensus.py`; writes nothing. |
| `eclcensus.py` | Where a title's area scripts keep their variables, censused on the C64 and on DOS separately, since the two ports load the save at different addresses. Everything comes out of the title's own files: the opcode tables from its `DUNGEON` through `newecl.py`'s self-modifying dispatch, the script base from the five `GOTO`s each script opens with, and the DOS blocks through `goldbox.dos_savegame.dax_blocks`. It answered `#192 (Convert a Curse of the Azure Bonds DOS save into a C64 one, which the importer refuses today)` step 0a: 21 of Curse's 25 scripts are **byte-identical** to the DOS `ECL<n>.DAX` blocks of the same id, so the two ports name one address set, and that set is `$4B00`-`$4DFF` with nothing at all in `$4900`-`$4AFF` -- the quest-flag page is `$4C20`-`$4CFF` in Curse and Silver Blades, not the `$4A20`-`$4AF8` Pool of Radiance's labels had been carried across as. `--compare` diffs the two ports' bytes, `--range` prints one row per address, `--sites` every statement naming one, and `--loadfiles` runs both the control-flow walk and a raw byte-pattern scan so the two can disagree. `eclflags.py` is the fuller reading and is Pool of Radiance's alone; this one asks any title and answers less about each hit. It prints no string of the game's as text. |
| `eclexitkinds.py` | Says how each of the 79 exits is reached: `edge` (14, entry 0's `$6DD5` gate), `square` (43, entry 1's `ONGOTO`), `edge+square` (11, both), `square-via-entry0` (6, entry 0's `ONGOTO` with no gate), `entry1-unconditional` (4), and `entryN` for the one dispatched off camping or loading rather than any square or edge (`ECL0B`'s `$A20F`) -- with the id and the `GEO` squares carrying it, whether it names the square by an `ONGOTO` arm or, as three of the four `entry1-unconditional` exits do, by masking `$C04F` into a variable and testing that with `COMPARE` and a conditional jump (`#255 (tools/areas/eclexitkinds.py misses a square exit whose id is tested by COMPARE rather than ONGOTO)`) -- and the statements on the route a player would notice: a menu, printed text, a `LOADCHAR`, a quest flag, a position write. It is the answer to "which handler" for `#207 (Run an exit's own handler before Fast Travel warps out)`, where an area pair names no unique one: thirteen script/target pairs are reached by more than one exit and all thirteen collapse once the exit is keyed by square and facing instead. Regenerated from the disks through `eclwalk.py`; prints no string of the game's as text. |
| `eclflags.py` | Every reference the thirty area scripts make to the quest-flag page `$4A00`-`$4AF8`, classified into reads and writes and keyed by address -- the regenerable replacement for `reports/quest-flags.md` (scratch, deleted), which was lost with its directory (`#136 (Thirty-two cited write-ups are gone, because the knowledge base pointed into gitignored scratch)`). It walks the scripts with `eclwalk.py` and knows from the instruction set which operand of each opcode is a destination, so `SAVE 250, [$4A04]` counts as a write and `COMPARE [$4A04], 250` does not; `sites 4A81` prints every instruction that touches one address, which is how a new quest gets read. `doc` regenerates `docs/151-quest-flags.md` whole. It prints no string as text -- the English in its last column is joined out of `goldbox/commissions.py`, so the table cannot drift from the code and none of the game's own words reach the repository. |
| `ecllist.py` | Read one area script: a run of its statements from any offset, the 6502 handler behind an ECL opcode, and which of its `COMBAT` statements a given saved game can actually reach -- the three things `#334 (The session driver cannot fight in Curse or Silver Blades, and says the party is not in a fight while it is standing on the combat floor)` kept doing by hand. `eclcensus.py` answers which addresses a title's scripts name and `--sites` prints the statements naming one; neither prints a run of statements, which is what every reading on that ticket wanted. `--at` takes the address a script runs at or its offset in the file; `--raw` dumps bytes, which is how an operand's **kind** byte gets read (`$00` immediate byte, `$01` byte variable, `$02` immediate word, `$03` word variable, `$80` string) and the kind is what says whether a store touches one byte or two. `--handler` disassembles an opcode's handler, found through `newecl.py`'s self-modifying dispatch rather than from an address anybody wrote down -- that is what settles "does `SAVE` into a byte variable clobber its neighbour" from the engine instead of from plausibility. `--combat --save` walks every arm of an entry's `ONGOTO` with a real `SAVEDBASH` in hand, evaluating each `COMPARE`/`IF` pair against the save's own bytes and branching both ways only where a value is genuinely unknown, so it answers "which square in this area fights *this* party": on every Silver Blades save specimen on this machine, arms 15 and 16 of `ECL10` reach `COMBAT` with **no** open decision. It prints no string of the game's as text. |
| `eclnpc.py` | Every reached `ADDNPC` in Pool of Radiance's thirty area scripts, with the `MON` record each one joins and the control-flow path in front of it -- the census `#533 (A joined NPC has no combat icon, and the editor draws the absence as a black rectangle)` needed to decide which joinable NPC a driven session can reach most cheaply. `census` walks each script through `eclwalk.py`'s `Script` class from its five entry `GOTO`s, so a listed statement is one the engine can reach, and resolves a computed record id through the script's own `GETTABLE` -- `ECL0B $9FEB` takes both operands from an eight-byte table at `$A23F`, the Training Hall's eight hirelings. It folds the second operand the way `DUNGEON` `$273D`-`$2759` does, `(n >> 1) | $80`, the byte stored at record `0x0B8`. `gaps` is the completeness check the census rests on: of the 3,481 bytes across the thirty scripts the walk never reaches, none decodes as an `ADDNPC`, so the twelve `census` found are all of them. `gate ECL0B 9FEB` walks backward from a statement over the same control-flow graph and prints every block that reaches it, entry included, which is how the reads and writes of the quest-flag page along a join's path get read block by block rather than guessed at. It prints the `MON` names, which are the game's own words, so its output belongs in a terminal and not in `docs/`, a README, a commit message or an issue. |
| `ecltext.py` | Decodes an area script's packed strings, so a screen capture can be matched to the statement that printed it -- what `#334 (The session driver cannot fight in Curse or Silver Blades, and says the party is not in a fight while it is standing on the combat floor)`'s script-VM read wanted, since `eclcensus.py` deliberately prints a string operand as its byte length and cannot answer which line a screen came from. Six-bit groups, most significant bit first, read out of the title's own `DUNGEON` at the handler its operand fetch jumps to for operand kind `$80`; a group of `$01`-`$1F` is OR'd with `$40` to land on `A`-`Z`, `$20`-`$3F` stands as itself, `$00` terminates. Checked on all three C64 titles. `--text` and `--find` print the game's own prose, so their output must not reach `docs/`, a README, a commit message or an issue; without `--text` it prints offsets and lengths only, which is safe anywhere. |
| `eclwalk.py` | Reads an area script statement by statement and prints, per exit, exactly what a fast travel skips. Every number it needs comes out of `DUNGEON` at run time -- the opcode handler tables, the operand count, and the three shapes `$1663` decodes -- so it cannot drift from the game. It walks each script from its five entry addresses following `GOTO`, `GOSUB`, `ONGOTO`, `ONGOSUB` and both arms of every `IF` rather than sweeping it linearly, because a linear sweep runs into the data tables opcode `$2A` indexes and turns them into nonsense; it reaches 98% of all 178035 bytes of the thirty scripts. Rebuilt for `docs/150-departing-prologues.md` after `analysis6/ecl6.py` (scratch, deleted), which decoded all thirty to 100% of every byte, went with the scratch directory (`#136 (Thirty-two cited write-ups are gone, because the knowledge base pointed into gitignored scratch)`). Prints a string operand as its length and never as text, because its output goes into a repository the game's words must not enter. |
| `exitreentry.py` | Runs an exit's own handler from outside the game, for `#207 (Run an exit's own handler before Fast Travel warps out)`: it teleports the party on to the exit square and re-enters `DUNGEON` where a step would have arrived -- `$0957` after a landed step, or `$0A4C` then `$0978` for the forward key off a map edge -- with the stack rebuilt from the depth `$03BF` records. Phases measure the idle stack at the command bar and in MOVE mode, the `NO` and `YES` answers at the Kobold Caves' exit, the second hop back indoors, and an edge exit from New Phlan. Captures go to the temp directory. |
| `exitroute.py` | Prints every statement on the route to one exit, from the entry `DUNGEON` dispatches through down to the `NEWECL`, marking what a player would notice -- a menu, printed text, a `LOADCHAR`, a quest flag, a position write, a fight. `eclexitkinds.py` counts those features; this shows the route they came from, which is how `ECL07 $A904` turned out to be the endgame battle with Tyranthraxus rather than six `SAVE`s and an exit. |
| `fasttravelpcwait.py` | Measures how long the program counter stays outside Fast Travel's windows, idle at 20 ms and 5 ms sampling and while the party walks, so the wait's time limit rests on how long a miss lasts rather than how often one happens, for `#152 (The Fast Travel button greys itself out for a second while the party stands still)`. Claims a pool slot, stages the Pool disks and a save (`--save`, default `PORSAVE11.D64`), boots and drives the game. Writes `wait.json` under `--out`. |
| `fasttravelrun.py` | Drives the **shipped** `automap.actions.FastTravel().run()` through a real `automap.target.ViceTarget` on a pool slot, for `#207 (Run an exit's own handler before Fast Travel warps out)`: boots `npc_party.d64`, warps from the Kobold Caves (area 13) to the East Window (area 27) through the production code path -- not `exitreentry.py`'s hand-rolled `reenter()` -- answers the handler's own `YES`/`NO` prompt on row 24 the way a player would, and checks that Princess Fatima was dropped from the roster exactly as walking out drops her. `--from-area`, `--to-area` and `--member` generalise the check; only the Kobold Caves case has a save that exercises it and has actually been run. `--disks` and `--out` take the disk directory and output directory as arguments rather than a hardcoded path, falling back to `$POR_DISKS` then `automap.paths.find_disks()`. The pool owns the emulator lifecycle throughout, torn down on every exit path including an exception. |
| `geomap.py` | Renders GEO map files as text floor plans, optionally with a save's party marked. `--find` is the anchor problem: given where the party stands but not which map, it reports every GEO where that square exists and the party is not inside a wall, narrowing 29 files to a handful. |
| `georesident.py` | How far the resident map block at `$0400` drifts from its `GEO` disk copy in a driven *Pool of Radiance* game, which is the measurement the tolerance in `automap.area.NEAR_ENOUGH` never had. It boots, loads a save and, for every step of a `--route`, asks the shipped `ResidentGeo.verdict` what the block is, records the distance to every map, and counts loads and stores into `$0400`-`$07FF` with two VICE checkpoints: the load count is the positive control and the store count is the measurement. Writes `run.jsonl` and one screenshot per step into `--out`, and never writes the player's disks. |
| `geowalk.py` | Walks a driven session to a square, routing breadth-first over the area's own `GEO` so it never asks the game to walk through a wall, and resuming when a scripted square interrupts with a message. Routing off the map the game itself loaded is what made a Curse shopping trip one walk rather than a search (`#32 (One Curse session, to get a party with items)`). |
| `loadfiles.py` | Every `LOADFILES` and `LOADPIECES` in a script with its immediate operands, which is the quick way to ask what an area loads and what it gives back. It is a byte-pattern scan and needs no `DUNGEON`; `eclwalk.py` is the fuller reading and knows what a statement is. This is what made `#156 (Warping from the Slums to New Phlan draws New Phlan with the Slums' walls)` a measurement rather than a disassembly, and it was written into a scratch directory (`cited/wallart/`) where a tool cannot be found and does not survive. |
| `newecl.py` | Finds a title's `NEWECL` handler and everything else a fast travel needs -- the tail to jump to, the key-wait window, the key fetcher, the mode flag, the disk byte, the cache slot and the two save-relative writes -- **by reading its own `DUNGEON`, `LINKER` and `LIBRARY` rather than by carrying an address across**. The script VM is found by its *self-modifying dispatch*: a `JSR` whose own two operand bytes are the target of `STA`s elsewhere in the same overlay, which each `DUNGEON` has exactly two of. Entry `$20` of the tables it builds is the handler, and the routine is then compared against Pool of Radiance's instruction for instruction with the relocations worked out from the operands, so a title whose handler is *not* the same routine says so instead of handing back a plausible address. It reproduces every one of Pool of Radiance's documented values, including the two windows that were measured from 400 program-counter samples of an idle party, and it took Curse from UNKNOWN to CONFIRMED and Silver Blades to PROBABLE in one command each. No address comes from a PRG header, which is `#17 (Are other overlay addresses off by $800, like the one just found?)`'s trap: `DUNGEON` runs at `$0800` and the three titles' headers claim `$1000`, `$3000` and `$4000`. |
| `postcom6dereads.py` | For #445 (The game's third fight outcome, THE PARTY RUNS AWAY, has never been seen on a screen): every read of $6DE2, $6DE3, $6DE6, $6DC6 and $6DE1 in Pool of Radiance's `POST.COM`, with eight 6502 instructions from each, the engine side of ECL00's tavern-brawl writes. Reads the player's C64 disks; writes nothing. |
| `reentrypoints.py` | Checks the five re-entry addresses `#207 (Run an exit's own handler before Fast Travel warps out)` rests on against the player's own `DUNGEON`, with no emulator: it **derives** `saved_sp` and `main_loop_return` from the bytes -- `NEWECL`'s tail is `LDX <saved_sp> / TXS / JMP <main loop>`, and the main loop's own self-modifying `JSR` is found by its unpatched `$FFFF` operand -- and checks the shape of `after_step` (which must call the committed `redraw`), `forward_key`, the main loop's `TSX / STX <saved_sp>`, and the wall test the edge-exit square choice rests on. Seven checks; exit status 1 if any fails, so a change to those constants can be gated on it. |
| `wallpins.py` | Watches `$49E7`-`$49E9` pin two wall pieces in Valhingen Graveyard and watches the next area draw them, which is `#179 (Warping out of Valhingen Graveyard or Valjevo Castle leaves two wall pieces unrelocated)` confirmed rather than argued. Three arrivals at Podol Plaza's own square with the party and the session held constant: warped in from the Slums, warped out of the graveyard with the fix's `$49E7` write dropped, and warped out with the whole of `newecl_writes` -- the faulty one is driven by building the write list here and removing one entry, never by reverting `automap/actions.py`, which several agents share. `--plan walkin` is the other half: one step west off the graveyard's edge to watch the game clear the pins itself, then the eight compass digits tried in turn to walk back in through the entrance `ECL1A`'s dispatch tables name. Captures go to the temp directory. |
| `windowsquare.py` | Warps to each wilderness window and tries to walk off the square wish ships in `goldbox/areas.py`, which is the only way to answer it: passability is in each window's own impassable-terrain table, and that table's address was in `reports/world-map.md` (scratch, deleted), which is lost (`#136 (Thirty-two cited write-ups are gone, because the knowledge base pointed into gitignored scratch)`). `$49C3`/`$49C4` are seeded (0, 0) before every warp, so an arrival on the table's square cannot be the stale one `#178 (Fast Travel to the wilderness leaves the party on whatever overland square it last stood on)` is about; the warp goes through the shipped `FastTravel.apply` rather than a hand-built write list, and each of the eight compass digits is pressed with the party put back on the arrival square first. **It answers the boat menu and presses again before calling a direction blocked** -- both boat landings re-open TAKE BOAT / STAY every time the party is put back on them, and the first run read seven of window 26's eight directions as impassable when what blocked them was its own unanswered dialogue. Captures go to `--out`, by default the tool's scratch directory. |
