# icons

Scripts for combat icons and portraits: the correspondence tables between the DOS and C64 art, and the tools that build, check and draw them.

| file | purpose |
|---|---|
| `amigaicons.py` | Measures the Amiga *Curse* and *Silver Blades* combat-icon art against DOS's own, which is what settled `#396 (Whether an Amiga Curse or Silver Blades record's combat-icon fields share DOS's own numbering is unmeasured)`. `--tables` reads the three tables the icon routine uses out of each executable's data hunk at a resolved address -- the sixteen-entry translation from a DOS pixel value to an Amiga palette entry, the six part codes the record's colour bytes are written through, and the `S`/`T` size letter. `--art` reads `CHEAD.TLB` and `CBODY.TLB` off the player's own disk (a `GLIB` container of five-bitplane tiles, not the `.dax` `goldbox/amiga_dax.py` reads) and compares every block against the same title's DOS `.DAX` through that table: the block ids are DOS's own and 171,604 pixels of 171,696 are DOS's own, the 92 that are not being the hat-and-plume highlight. `--census` prints all 21 Amiga specimens' four icon fields and checks each `(head, body, size)` names blocks the libraries actually hold. Every disk and executable is opened read-only. `docs/199-amiga-combat-icons.md` is the write-up. |
| `dosfigures.py` | Converts a DOS save folder into a C64 save disk on which **every character keeps his own combat figure**, which is what `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)` is about. It hands `goldbox.dos_codec.new_save` the C64's own option tables rather than a composed default, so the six icons on the disk are the shipped conversion's work; it then composes the same six a second time through `goldbox.iconparts.IconParts.dos_icon` and compares, and exits non-zero if the disk holds anything else -- the difference between proving the composition and proving the button. `--png` draws each character's DOS figure in his record's own EGA colours beside the C64 figure he converts to, both poses of both, which is how a row of Donald's table is judged on a real character rather than on a sheet. `--mixed-png` draws the nine rows a small character has to wear from the large list. The disks come from `$POR_DISKS` then `automap.paths.find_disks()` and are read, never written; the output goes under the temp directory. |
| `dosiconstage.py` | Sets a DOS party's combat-figure bytes, to give a conversion something to say, for `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)`. The one DOS party watched being written holds `icon_head` 0 and `icon_body` 0 for all six characters, so this writes `icon_head`, `icon_body` and the `icon_colours` pairs into the `CHRDAT` files of a `--folder` and `--slot`, and nothing else. |
| `dosicontitles.py` | Answers whether a DOS Curse of the Azure Bonds or Secret of the Silver Blades record numbers its combat art the way a DOS Pool of Radiance record does (`#330 (A converted Curse or Silver Blades figure is composed through Pool of Radiance's icon table, which nobody has checked transfers)`), which decides whether one correspondence table can serve all three. Two halves. The art: it unpacks every block of each title's `CHEAD.DAX` and `CBODY.DAX` and compares each block id against the reference title's, so "the same art in the same order" is a count -- Curse is 184 of 184 byte-identical and Silver Blades 182, the two it re-drew reported as the option, size and pose they draw with how many 4-bit pixels moved and which named parts each side uses, rather than as a copy of the art. The code, `--code`: each title's own ICON menu wrap constants read out of `GAME.OVR` at the record displacement `goldbox/dos_port.py` gives that title -- 13 heads and 31 bodies in all three -- and the importer that reads the previous title's record, which copies `icon_head`, `icon_body`, `size` and the six `icon_colours` straight across with no table in between. Pools of Darkness is available with `--titles` and its blocks fail the 4-bit image test, so its art is a different encoding and is counted rather than compared. Reads the archives and writes only what `--json` names. |
| `dosmixedicon.py` | Finds, or makes, a **small** DOS character whose row in `iconproposal.yaml` names a C64 option only the large list has -- six weapon rows and three head rows of Donald's table land past the small lists of 28 and 14, and whether such a figure reads as a whole figure on the screen is a look that needs a specimen. `--census` reads every `.SAV` and `.CHA` under the DOS corpora and reports the ones already on such a row: on 2026-09-05, **2 of 372 records**, both of them the same Pools of Darkness character in two copies of the same party, so no Pool of Radiance party on this machine has one and it has to be staged. `--stage` copies a party out and rewrites the `icon_head` `0x0BD` and `icon_body` `0x0BE` of its small characters, leaving `size` `0x0C0` and the colours alone, so the only difference from the party it came from is which figure a small character asks for. Its neighbour `dosiconstage.py` is a different job -- six different figures for a party that has one -- and this one is about a single row of the table. |
| `dosnibbles.py` | Which of a DOS part's two colours covers more of it, the main or the highlight, counted in pixels over every block of `CHEAD.DAX` and `CBODY.DAX` at both sizes and both poses. A DOS `icon_colours` byte holds two 4-bit colours a part and the C64 keeps one, so a conversion has to pick a nibble: `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)` picks the low one as the main colour, and this says whether that is true and how often the choice makes any difference at all. `--per-option` gives one line per `CBODY` option. |
| `dosportraitparty.py` | Opens every character's sheet in DOS Pool of Radiance and **names the face drawn**, matching the frame against every `HEAD<n>.DAX`/`BODY<n>.DAX` block the way `portraitshot.py` does one. It exists because a conversion that wrote character 1's portrait into all six records passes a one-sheet check: the acceptance question in `#57 (Convert the character portrait across ports)` is per character. `--c64` converts a C64 save disk into the slot from nothing through `goldbox.dos_codec.new_dos_save`, so the run drives the shipped import rather than staged bytes. **The DOS sheet has no next-character key** -- sixteen were pressed and the art never changed -- so `--first K` swaps two of the six 41-byte party entries at file offset 12809 of `SAVGAM<slot>.DAT` and boots again, which reorders the marching order and touches no record. `--probe` presses keys from a sheet and identifies the art after each; `--sequence` does the same from the map, photographing rather than identifying. |
| `iconcorrespond.py` | Lays the two ports' combat-icon option lists side by side and scores the pairing, which is the thing `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)` had to know first: DOS body `n` is **not** C64 weapon `n`. It reads `CHEAD.DAX` and `CBODY.DAX` out of the DOS game directory and `SPELLE64`, `SPELLN64` and `CHARPIC00` off `POOL3.D64`, renders every option of both ports to a 24x24 ink mask, and scores every pairing over a small alignment window so that a figure drawn a pixel lower cannot read as a different figure. The counts answer most of it — DOS offers 32 bodies and 14 heads in each of its two sizes, against the C64's 28 weapons and 14 heads small and 35 and 23 large — and the scores finish it: the same index is the best match once or twice in each list, which is chance, and the highest overlap of all 1120 large pairings is 0.78, for the plain unarmed figure that is obviously the same design in both ports. `--catalogue` prints one port's whole list as ASCII and `--show N` puts one DOS figure beside its best C64 match; `--png` writes a contact sheet of both lists in colour under the temp directory, which is the output a person actually needs, because choosing which C64 figure each DOS one becomes is a decision rather than a measurement. |
| `iconpoke.py` | Gives every character on a save disk a different combat figure, composed the way the game's own ICON menu composes one, so a fight can tell each character's shape apart from the party's (`#184 (A converted combat icon's colours are proven in the game and its shapes are not)`, `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)`). Edits the disk in place; point it at a copy under the temp directory. |
| `iconproposal.py` | Holds the **proposed** table for `#130 (A converted DOS party arrives with six identical combat figures, not its own)` -- which C64 weapon each of the 32 DOS bodies becomes, which C64 head each of the 14 DOS heads, and which of the C64's eight colours each of EGA's sixteen -- and draws every row so the proposal can be judged by looking: the DOS figure in its record's own colours on the left, recoloured the way `GAME.OVR:0x1E55C` does it, and the proposed C64 figure on the right in the colours the same record converts to, with the alternatives beside it where a second option looked as close. Nothing here converts a save; the tables are Donald's to approve and move into `goldbox/` afterwards. `--colours` takes a record's six `icon_colours` bytes, `--kind head` and `--size small` the other three sheets. **The tables are `iconproposal.yaml`, beside this file, which Donald edits by hand** -- one row per DOS figure with its proposed C64 number, its alternatives and the descriptive comment naming what the DOS figure shows. `--markdown` generates the document fresh from that YAML every time -- one image per figure, a row per DOS figure and a numbered gallery of every C64 option to judge against -- so the document is never the thing edited. There used to be a `--from-markdown` that read a hand-edited document back; it is gone, because the YAML file replaces the round trip it existed for. **`--title`** picks whose own DOS art `--markdown` and `--png` draw -- Pool of Radiance, Curse of the Azure Bonds or Secret of the Silver Blades, so Donald can look at all three tables rather than only Pool of Radiance's (`#330 (A converted Curse or Silver Blades figure is composed through Pool of Radiance's icon table, which nobody has checked transfers)`). A title other than Pool of Radiance's own document is checked against Pool of Radiance's art block for block and every row says plainly whether it is the same figure or this title's own redrawn one -- Curse's document says at the top that every row is the same and none needs judging, and Secret of the Silver Blades' names its two known redrawn rows and shows the differing picture beside each (`#335 (Two combat-figure rows describe Pool of Radiance's art, and Silver Blades draws those two options differently)`). **Both sides now come off the named title's own art**, and that was not a formality: the C64 half used to be drawn off `POOL3.D64` whatever `--title` said, on the strength of `SPELLE64` being the identical 1882 bytes in all three. It is -- but the glyphs those option tables *name* live in `CHARPIC00`, and Silver Blades redraws three of its 253, so C64 weapon 13 at either size and large heads 8 and 13 are different pictures on a `SILVER-*.D64`. `--compare-c64` is that measurement, option by option and pixel by pixel across all three titles' disks; the disk itself is found through `gamedisks.yaml` and then by looking for the side that carries `SPELLE64`, `SPELLN64` and `CHARPIC00` together, which is `POOL3.D64`, `CURSE_A.D64` and `SILVER-1.D64` here. With none of the named title's own disks on the machine, `--markdown` writes the document with the DOS side complete and no C64 figure at all, saying so where each one would have been, because a document that stands another game's art in is worse than one that shows none. |
| `iconproposal.yaml` | The proposal's three tables -- DOS weapon to C64 weapon, DOS head to C64 head, EGA colour to C64 colour -- as data, so `iconproposal.py` draws them rather than holding them as Python literals. Comfortable to edit by hand: one line per row, the C64 number, any alternatives, and a comment naming the DOS figure. Our judgement, not the game's data, so it is committed. Also holds an `overrides:` section, one per title that redrew its own art. Donald picked the first row on 2026-09-05 -- Secret of the Silver Blades' DOS head 10 to C64 head 2, for `#335 (Two combat-figure rows describe Pool of Radiance's art, and Silver Blades draws those two options differently)`; the body of that pair is still open. `goldbox.iconparts.dos_icon_tables` reads the section by title and is unaffected when a title names none of its own -- and **no caller passes it a title yet**, so an override reaches a proposal document and not a conversion. |
| `iconredrawn.py` | Draws the two combat-figure options Secret of the Silver Blades re-drew, so `#335 (Two combat-figure rows describe Pool of Radiance's art, and Silver Blades draws those two options differently)` can be decided by looking rather than from a table of pixel counts. Three figures per row, side by side and scaled up: the DOS option as Pool of Radiance draws it -- the drawing every row of `iconproposal.yaml` was chosen against -- the same option as Silver Blades draws it, and the C64 figure the row names today, composed the way a conversion composes it. Both poses of each, a sentence saying what a Silver Blades player would see differently, and a numbered gallery of the C64 options that show what Silver Blades shows, so the answer can be a number. Neither end is assumed: the mapping is read out of the YAML at run time and which options diverge is measured block for block the way `dosicontitles.py` measures it, so the page cannot describe a row that has changed or a divergence that has gone. The output is one self-contained HTML file with the figures embedded, and it is the game's own art -- it goes under the temp directory and is never committed. |
| `iconreverse.py` | Holds the **proposed** table for `#320 (A C64 party converted to DOS arrives with no combat figure at all, because the table only runs one way)` -- which DOS body each of the C64's 63 weapon options becomes and which DOS head each of its 37 head options -- and draws every row so it can be judged by looking, the way `iconproposal.py` draws the table going the other way. Three modes beyond the drawing. `--coverage` says how each of the 100 rows is decided: **54 are forced**, because exactly one DOS option becomes that C64 one in `iconproposal.yaml` and reversing it is the only answer that returns a player their own figure on a round trip; 14 are a pick among the DOS options the forward table merged, where any pick round-trips; and 32 are a C64 figure DOS has none of, which is where Donald's eye is actually needed. `--census` reads every combat icon on the player's own C64 disks back into menu choices through `goldbox.iconparts.IconParts.recognise` -- 222 icons over Pool of Radiance, Curse and Silver Blades, 35 distinct shapes, all 35 named. `--markdown` writes the document Donald reads: one row per C64 option with the figure beside the DOS figure it becomes, the alternatives after it, whether the row is forced or a judgement, and the comment saying why -- generated fresh from the YAML every time, so the document is never the thing edited. The pictures are the game's own art and go under the temp directory. |
| `iconreverse.yaml` | The reverse proposal's five tables -- C64 large weapon and head, C64 small weapon and head, and C64 colour to DOS colour pair -- as data, so `iconreverse.py` draws them rather than holding them as Python literals. The key is a **C64** option and the value a DOS one, which is the opposite way round from `iconproposal.yaml`, and both size sections are complete lists rather than exceptions, because C64 large weapon 3 and C64 small weapon 3 are different drawings out of different tables. Each row carries the proposed DOS number, any alternatives, and a comment saying what the C64 figure shows and what decided the row. Our judgement, not the game's data, so it is committed. `goldbox.iconparts.c64_icon_tables` reads it, off `goldbox.assets.asset_path` so a frozen build finds it the way `#315 (A frozen Wish cannot convert a combat figure, because the table it needs lives outside the package)` fixed for the forward table, and `IconParts.dos_icon_from_c64` and `goldbox.dos_codec.c64_party` both call it. It carries a per-title `overrides:` section too, the mirror of `iconproposal.yaml`'s own, for `#452 (A Silver Blades combat figure does not survive a round trip through the C64, because the reverse table has no per-title rows)` -- but no caller passes `c64_party` a title yet, so a Silver Blades character round-tripped through the C64 today still comes home reading only the base table. 
| `iconrowproof.py` | Stages one combat-figure row into a DOS party, converts it, and says what each character arrived holding -- the proof a row of `iconproposal.yaml` reaches a *converted character* rather than only a proposal document. Reading the row proves nothing on its own: the table is merged from four levels, the merge takes a title and a size, and a caller that forgets either gets the base answer and a complete, plausible figure that is not the one the player made. So this copies a save folder or a named specimen into a directory of its own, rewrites the `icon_head` and `icon_body` of the characters of one size, converts through `goldbox.dos_codec.convert_save` -- the entry point the import dialog uses -- and reads each C64 icon back into the menu choices that drew it through `IconParts.recognise`, printing the option numbers and the part classes the figure draws. `--control` converts a second time with the title's own `overrides:` section ignored and prints both, so a row's effect is a difference rather than an assertion. `--home` reads each arriving icon straight back into DOS through `IconParts.dos_icon_from_c64`, once through `goldbox.iconparts.c64_icon_tables()` with no title -- what `goldbox.dos_codec.c64_party` still builds today -- and once with the title this run staged for, so a round trip is a comparison against what was staged rather than a reading of either table: on `#452 (A Silver Blades combat figure does not survive a round trip through the C64, because the reverse table has no per-title rows)`, staging head 10 and body 11 onto an engine-written Silver Blades party and running `--home` shows every large character coming home as head 4 with no title and head 10 with one, and the one small character coming home as body 0 with no title and body 11 with one -- the exact two readings the issue opens with, off the player's own `SILVER-1.D64` and specimen `WISH-SPEC-ssb-299-engine-resave`. The specimen is copied, never edited in place, and nothing is written outside the staging directory, which belongs under the temp directory. |
| `iconsheet.py` | Renders every icon the program ships at the sizes it is actually seen at, through the same painting code the map and the roster use. Exists because a table of icon *names* is how `hat-wizard` got chosen and how it turned out to read as a shark's fin at 13 pixels. |
| `iconswing.py` | Answers where a combat icon's **second** nine screen codes go, which `#184 (A converted combat icon's colours are proven in the game and its shapes are not)` had left open: no reading taken at a command bar has ever found them on the combat floor, because they are fetched while a character takes its turn and gone before the game asks for the next command. Two instruments. In a fight it drives with `Session.melee_turn`, so the party strikes rather than passing -- 35 blows on 42 turns against 0 on 80 for the run in `docs/174-combat-figures-in-the-running-game.md` -- and arms VICE load checkpoints on two levels at once: the save's own codes at `$4BE0 + slot * 36`, which are read exactly nine times a window a fight and never again, and the bitmaps `COM.PREP $122C` expands them into at `$9BE8 + slot * 162`, whose second-pose block is read 72 bytes at a time, once a turn, for the character the camera is centred on. It reads that table at every command bar and scores all eighteen expanded glyphs against `CHARPIC00` for the save's own codes. `--camp` takes the game to ENCAMP > ALTER > ICON instead, where the editor draws four figures labelled `READY` and `ACTION` -- the game's own names for the two poses -- and scores every 3x3 block of nine consecutive codes on that screen against both poses of every occupied slot, through the editor's own character set at `$D000` and the monitor's `ram` bank. Findings in `docs/186-ready-and-action.md`; output under the temp directory, never in the repository. |
| `inkcount.py` | Counts an icon's ink pixels and how many connected pieces it comes apart into, at the size the program actually draws it. The two numbers `docs/136-condition-badges.md` grades every condition badge by, and it reproduces all of them — the rig that produced them originally lived in a session and had to be rebuilt to add two rows. Ink says how much of the glyph survives (`invisible` was dropped at 55); pieces says whether it survives as one thing (`hat-wizard`'s brim stopped touching its cone and it read as a shark's fin). |
| `portraitdraw.py` | Which C64 title's character sheet draws a portrait at all, for `#300 (A Curse or Silver Blades party imported to the C64 arrives with no sheet portrait, because the creation menu is read only off a POOL<n>.D64)`: finds each overlay's load address by scoring its own jump targets, the loader's two entries by their code, and `ANIMATE`'s five draw entries out of that title's own load-address table, then counts the calls to each over every file on every side. It works on a title nobody has mapped, which is what let it answer the question for Curse and Silver Blades. |
| `portraitmenu.py` | Re-derives the twenty-six numbers `goldbox/portraits.py` stores as `POOL_OF_RADIANCE_MENU` -- the fourteen head and twelve body art ids of Pool of Radiance's character-creation menu, in menu order -- by reading them off the C64's `GEN` and DOS's `START.EXE` with the same run-finder the conversion uses, and prints each as the Python literal the stored block is written in. `--check` compares both readings with the stored block and exits 1 on any disagreement, printing both tables; that is the test for a release whose menu differs from the one read on 2026-09-06, which is the one thing that would make the stored table wrong for somebody's disks. Nothing but the ids leaves either file. Donald, 2026-09-06: *"Just pull them from each title so you can cross reference them. Then you don't need the disks at all."* |
| `portraitshot.py` | Photographs a DOS character's sheet portrait under DOSBox and **says which art it drew** -- the drawn pixels are matched against every `HEAD<n>.DAX`/`BODY<n>.DAX` block rendered from the player's own files, so the answer is an art id rather than a screenshot to squint at. That is what turned `#57 (Carry the character portrait across ports)`'s one-based menu position from an argument into a measurement: position 12 draws `$39`, and `$39` is the twelfth entry of the creation table. `--rewrite` puts a slot's own records through the writer and leaves the saved game alone, `--template` does the opposite, and `--words 49FF=3` patches saved-game words -- the three handles that isolated a faceless converted party to one word of `SAVGAM<slot>.DAT` rather than to the records. Stages its own copy of the game tree and opens no window. |
